---
title: Prune retained history
description: Schedule bounded cleanup without treating retention as a deletion deadline.
---

Run cleanup separately from delivery workers. Neither `worker.tick()` nor `worker.run()` calls `prune()`.

The default retention window is seven days. Expired events become eligible for deletion; retention is not a guaranteed deletion deadline.

## Run a bounded cleanup process

Configure the database, schema, and encryption key as in [worker setup](/operations/workers). Run the checked entrypoint from the repository root:

```sh
node packages/good-webhooks/examples/operations/cleanup.ts
```

Each run calls `prune()` sequentially for up to 20 batches or 30 seconds. Override these budgets with `WEBHOOK_CLEANUP_MAX_BATCHES` and `WEBHOOK_CLEANUP_MAX_DURATION_MS`.

</include>

Each batch deletes at most 100 expired events, along with their deliveries and attempts. A positive batch continues the loop. Zero deletions, a signal, or an exhausted budget stops it. The time budget is checked between batches, so an in-progress batch can exceed that budget within the database timeout bounds.

The process closes its pool before reporting `webhooks.cleanup.finished`, including batch count, deletion count, and stop reason.

## Schedule enough cleanup capacity

Use your host's scheduler and avoid overlapping runs. Monitor deletion counts and the age of expired history.

Twenty batches every minute can remove at most 2,000 expired events per minute. Query time, locks, and active leases can lower that capacity. Choose a cadence whose observed capacity exceeds the rate at which history expires.

`no_progress` means the last batch deleted nothing. Locks or active delivery leases can defer otherwise expired events. It does not prove that all eligible history is gone.

Budget exhaustion emits `webhooks.cleanup.budget_exhausted` and still exits successfully. Alert on repeated exhaustion and on nonzero exits. Exhaustion alone does not prove a backlog remains, since the final batch may have removed all eligible events.

## Keep business idempotency separate

Pruning removes replay data and releases the deleted events' publication idempotency keys. If your business workflow needs longer-lived deduplication, retain those keys in application storage.

Cleanup operates across every scope in the configured delivery database and schema. It does not delete Better Auth management records or receiver-owned receipts.
