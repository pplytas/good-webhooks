Four other ways to send webhooks from an application, checked against their own documentation, repositories, and pricing pages on 27 October 2026. The [sources](#sources) are listed at the end. Open an [issue](https://github.com/pplytas/good-webhooks/issues) if a claim is out of date.

| Option                                                    | What it is                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Svix](https://www.svix.com)                              | A hosted webhook service with an open-source Rust server that covers part of it.                                |
| [Outpost](https://hookdeck.com/outpost)                   | A Go event-destinations gateway by Hookdeck, self-hosted or managed, that sends to webhooks and message queues. |
| [Convoy](https://getconvoy.io)                            | A Go webhook gateway with a dashboard, self-hosted or cloud, with paid features behind a license key.           |
| [pg-boss](https://github.com/timgit/pg-boss) plus `fetch` | A PostgreSQL job queue for Node.js plus about 50 lines of your own that sign and send each request.             |

Notes on the table:

- The default Good Webhooks retry schedule is the one Svix publishes.
- Svix's open-source server caps a `Retry-After` wait at twice the scheduled delay, and the hosted service documents neither `Retry-After` nor `410`.
- Endpoint management with the [Better Auth plugin](/docs/better-auth) lives in your Better Auth database, whatever its engine, and delivery stays on PostgreSQL.
- "Not found" in a cell means the feature appears in neither the documentation nor the source on the check date.

## What Good Webhooks does not have

- **A dashboard.** Svix's hosted service and Convoy ship one, and here you build admin pages on [`deliveries.list()`](/docs/operations/retries-and-replay#inspect-deliveries).
- **A receiver portal.** Svix's hosted service, Outpost, and Convoy's paid plans give endpoint owners a UI, and here the [Better Auth routes](/docs/better-auth) give signed-in users endpoint management without a UI.
- **Transformations.** Svix runs JavaScript per endpoint and Convoy on broker sources, and here the validated payload is sent as is.
- **mTLS to receivers.** Svix and Convoy offer it on Enterprise plans.
- **Rate limiting to receivers.** Svix and Convoy accept a per-endpoint rate, and here each endpoint has only a [`maxInFlight`](/docs/reference/delivery#deliverysettings) cap of 2 by default.
- **Storage other than PostgreSQL.** Delivery needs PostgreSQL 16, 17, or 18, and nothing else is supported.
- **A hosted service.** Svix, Outpost, and Convoy each run one.
- **Ordering guarantees.** Svix sells FIFO endpoints on Enterprise and pg-boss has a strict FIFO policy per key, and here delivery is [at least once with no order](/docs/concepts/delivery-guarantees), so receivers compare versions.
- **Senders in other languages.** The sender is TypeScript on Node.js 24 or later, while receivers in any language verify with any Standard Webhooks library.

The [roadmap](https://github.com/pplytas/good-webhooks/blob/main/docs/roadmap.md) marks the dashboard, portal, transformations, mTLS, other delivery databases, and a hosted service as not planned, and a per-endpoint rate limit as a candidate after 0.6.0.

## When to pick which

- **Svix** when you want a hosted service with a receiver portal, an SLA, and SDKs in nine languages, and you accept a per-message bill and an outbox of your own for atomicity.
  - Its open-source server is the choice when you want that API on your own PostgreSQL without the portal.
- **Outpost or Convoy** when several services in different languages publish events, or you need queue destinations (Outpost) or broker ingestion with a dashboard (Convoy), and you can run a Go service with Redis and more.
  - Convoy is Elastic License 2.0, and its community edition allows 1 user and 2 projects.
- **pg-boss plus `fetch`** when you send to a few endpoints you control, nobody registers their own, and the last attempt is all the history you need.
  - The 50 lines grow with the Standard Webhooks checklist: SSRF checks, `410`, `Retry-After`, secret rotation, attempt history, and replay by event ID.
- **Good Webhooks** when a TypeScript app on PostgreSQL lets users or organizations register endpoints, you want each event committed with your data and no new service, and you can run a worker and build any UI yourself.
